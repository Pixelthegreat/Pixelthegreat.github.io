&emsp;The next day was fine, though I needed more room for my thoughts than what I could get. As I was walking out of the classroom, Mr. Berry called my name. I couldn't fathom why. I was cooperative. I was attentive. Actually, I wasn't very attentive. Maybe that was why. I turned around slowly and walked over to him.

&emsp;"So, Mr. Rutten," he said with assertiveness.

&emsp;"I am he."

&emsp;"There's been some rumors."

&emsp;"What kind of rumors?" I tried to sound enthused.

&emsp;"They say there's been a killer on the loose."

&emsp;"The Lakefront Killer?"

&emsp;"Exactly. It's been ravaging the..."

&emsp;"... Lakefront. Yeah, I know. Something about old man Marley, lack of an autopsy report, uh, yeah."

&emsp;"Well you probably don't know that..."

&emsp;"... The police couldn't tell what hit him. Before you say anything, yes, I am fully aware of, and prepared for the danger. Now, if you'd excuse me, I would like some lunch."

&emsp;He looked a bit disappointed.

&emsp;"Fine, you can leave now."

&emsp;Among the sea of kids in the hall, there was someone jumping up and down. Though I could only see his head bob up a few times, it was clearly Mike. Whatever message he was trying to send, I could not pick up at that distance. I was planning on avoiding Mike and Josh that day, and yet, I still found myself sitting beside them. As I sat down with my tray of food, Mike immediately slammed his fist on the table.

&emsp;"Don't eat the mystery meat! Have an apple!" Before I could react, he was holding an apple above my tray.

&emsp;"It's fine, whatever it is I can tolerate."

&emsp;"You sure about that?" Josh said. They both stared at me intently, like two desperate pigeons.

&emsp;"Okay, okay, I'll take the apple!"

&emsp;With that, Mike dropped the apple on the tray.

&emsp;"Whoa now, you don't need to be so aggressive about it..."

&emsp;I looked to my left and to my right, but they were both gone again. A feeling began to sink onto my chest. I didn't want to look up, but I couldn't help it. Jenna, walking our way. I took a sigh, braced myself. Again, she took the seat opposite of mine. Again, she sat down in a rough overconfident manner. As usual, she wore her lumpy purse around her shoulder.

&emsp;"You're really gonna try this?" I asked. She sighed, and her posture tightened. She looked around.

&emsp;"I'm... sorry..." she said. It was awkward, stuttering, quiet.

&emsp;"For..."

&emsp;"For saying all of that!" She blurted out. "I'm sorry, okay?"

&emsp;I wanted to press her more, but I thought for a moment. I thought for a long moment. I looked at her. She was looking away. Her face was red, and her arms crossed.

&emsp;"Okay." She glanced over as I said this. "Seriously." I held out my hand. Reluctantly, she shook it.

&emsp;"Can you just... Can you please not tell anyone about... What happened... Yesterday? About what you saw?"

&emsp;I looked at her desperate, pleading eyes. "Okay." At this she sighed, and the tension seemed to leave her. She sat quiet and still, staring down at the table. Mike and Josh hadn't returned. I felt obligated to say something.

&emsp;"So uhm, what kinds of things do you do?"

&emsp;She looked up with a relaxed, smug face.

&emsp;"Well, I'm top of the cheerleaders, the teachers love me. I'm adored, hated. All the same."

&emsp;"No, I mean like, what do you _do_, outside of school?"

&emsp;"Well," she looked down. I waited for what became no answer. In an effort to get something out of her, I started listing things I was at least vaguely interested in.

&emsp;"Music? Art? Writing? Miniature golf?"

&emsp;I watched her closely as I spoke. Her eyes seemed to react, but she still said nothing.

&emsp;"Uh, astronomy, perhaps?"

&emsp;Her head tilted up a bit, and her eyes dashed over to me.

&emsp;"Really?"

&emsp;She went red again.

&emsp;"It... It's nothing, really. It's just a hobby."

&emsp;"That's fine by me. I enjoy the study."

&emsp;"Heh, you should see Jason! He doesn't even know the Earth is round!"

&emsp;"Who's Jason?"

&emsp;"Oh uh, he's over there, with the jocks."

&emsp;"Huh, sorry about that. I don't really know many people here."

&emsp;We sat still again for a moment. She cleared her throat.

&emsp;"Well, I should be going," she said as she stood up.

&emsp;"Oh, okay. Good talk?" She was walking away towards her two friends as I spoke. I sat for a moment and processed what had just happened. Maybe she wasn't so bad. Even so, I wanted that to be the end of it.

&emsp;"That was really weird," Mike said as he popped out from the table. He and Josh struggled out, but they eventually recomposed and sat down next to me again.

&emsp;"Thomas, do you need help?" Josh asked.

&emsp;"What do you mean?" I said.

&emsp;"Never mind, stupid question."

&emsp;"Anyways," Mike began in that theatrically menacing voice I had heard only yesterday. "Get ready for the plot twist of the century."

&emsp;"I thought that was when Y2K ended up doing nothing."

&emsp;"No no no, I swear this is gonna blow your mind!"

&emsp;"Oh, alright. Hit me."

&emsp;They both leaned in.

&emsp;"Today, someone found _this_ by the docks!"

&emsp;Josh held up a little pin with the school logo printed on. "They just gave these out a few days ago."

&emsp;"Do you know what this means?"

&emsp;"No, not really."

&emsp;"Really? Well, it must mean that the Lakefront Killer is a student of Mooreville High!"

&emsp;"_Or_ maybe it could mean that students here frequent the docks. Case closed."

&emsp;"You know what... Uhm... Good point."

&emsp;"Dude, I told you that one didn't make sense."

&emsp;"You definitely did not."

&emsp;"Did too."

&emsp;"Did not."

&emsp;As they bickered, I glanced over at Jenna. She was talking with those two other girls. If anything, it seemed more productive than this.

&emsp;"Guys, could you stop for a moment?"

&emsp;"Yeah, sure. What's up?" They both spoke in near perfect unison.

&emsp;"Does Jenna... Does she have any friends?"

&emsp;"Well, Lila and Rona, of course." Mike said. "Though, I've never really seen her hang out with anyone else."

&emsp;"What about the jocks?"

&emsp;"Oh, trust me, they find her just as annoying as we do."

&emsp;"Huh." She was strange. Quite strange.

&emsp;We arrived home, and my mother immediately left for something I couldn't care to remember. In the meantime, I began to unpack everything left. It wasn't a lot, just as much as our little car could fit. Admittedly, I didn't have to do it, but I didn't really have anything better to do.

&emsp;As I went through everything, I stopped at an old beat up cardboard box. There was an elaborate design wrapping the whole thing, yet no useful words could be distinguished. I opened it slowly. I removed the needless inner packaging. Now, I could see a small, amateur but functional telescope buried within. It was mine . I hadn't used it in a long time. I hadn't used it since the first time we moved.

&emsp;I carried it to the kitchen window and held it up. Though the lens was dusty and beat up, a picture could be made. Certainly a difficult one, but a picture nonetheless. Astronomy. I made a connection with Jenna over astronomy. Mike and Josh couldn't understand it. Somehow I found that in her. I swear, it was a shot in the dark, no more. I hated her. I thought I did. Maybe I didn't. Not so much. Maybe she wasn't so bad.
