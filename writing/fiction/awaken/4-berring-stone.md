# Awaken, Part 4: Berring Stone
_by Elliot Kohlmyer, October 29th, 2026_

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">_Heed unto the tales untold_</span>

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">_With'ring wood from times of old_</span>

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">_Cursed wretched beastly thing_</span>

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">_Shown the mirror scaled and winged_</span>

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">_Broken face so young and bright_</span>

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">_Kill by dusk and die by light_</span>

&emsp;"Are you alright?"

&emsp;"Oh, yeah, I'm fine."

&emsp;"Are you uh, are you leaving now, too?"

&emsp;"I don't know."

&emsp;"Got time to kill?"

&emsp;"Yep."

&emsp;I got up from the couch and motioned for her to follow. I stopped at a half open box, and pulled out a telescope. I blew off some dust.

&emsp;"We can use this."

&emsp;She stared blankly.

&emsp;"For stargazing."

&emsp;"Oh, okay..." She blushed.

&emsp;"Mhmm."

&emsp;We sat just outside the porch and set things up. Well, it was mostly me, but Jenna watched with curiosity. I looked up for a moment. The sky was clear and alive, though the trees covered a lot of it. I took one last look and focused the lens.

&emsp;"Alright, I'm ready. Why don't you take a look?"

&emsp;She looked in, and panned around in awe. She shortly turned to the branches of a tree, still looking through, and jumped. She began to chuckle. I began to chuckle.

&emsp;"It's a shame we've only got this small patch."

&emsp;Her eyes widened. "I know somewhere better!" She stood up and looked around the yard, stopping at the little opening in the back. I grabbed the telescope as quickly as I could. She grabbed my hand and pulled me along as I tried to keep up. We ran for a bit until we reached the opening, revealing the lake and a wide open view of the sky.

&emsp;"This is much better..."

&emsp;"Impressed eh?"

&emsp;"Don't let it get to your head. How did you even think to come here?"

&emsp;"I sorta like to hang out by the lake. Sometimes."

&emsp;We sat down. I placed down the telescope.

&emsp;"Do you wanna use this anymore?"

&emsp;"Nah, this is nice."

&emsp;Jenna pointed up to the sky, at what looked like a bright ship among a quiet sea.

&emsp;"Andromeda."

&emsp;"Oh, wow! I didn't know you could see it from here."

&emsp;"You can see lots of things from here."

&emsp;"How did you spot that so fast?"

&emsp;"I just know." She looked at me with a smug face.

&emsp;"And yet, you've never used a telescope before?"

&emsp;"I did just now."

&emsp;"Fair enough."

&emsp;"I never get the cool stuff. Just star maps."

&emsp;"Better than nothing."

&emsp;She began to lie down but stopped and jolted back up.

&emsp;"Ach..."

&emsp;"Something wrong?"

&emsp;"No, I just got poked by my purse."

&emsp;"Must be a sharp purse."

&emsp;"Heheh, you betcha!"

&emsp;I looked down and saw that the purse was slightly unzipped, and a faint glow came from inside.

&emsp;"What on earth do you keep in there?"

&emsp;She looked down for a moment and then zipped it up quickly.

&emsp;"Nothing. Nothing much."

&emsp;"I saw something glowing."

&emsp;"Glow in the dark."

&emsp;"What is... Are you okay?"

&emsp;"Why are you being so nosy?"

&emsp;"I'm... I'm sorry, I just... Two days ago, I saw you, behind the school..."

&emsp;"I told you not to bring it up!" Her face was as red as an apple. I sat for a moment, petrified.

&emsp;"Please, just tell me. You can trust me."

&emsp;She sighed deeply. She stared at the ground. She opened her purse, revealing a small ornamented box glowing a faint blue. She tensed.

&emsp;"I was told, I would die if I held on to it for too long. That I would turn into some sort of... beast." I quietly sat, thoughts scattered. She lightened somewhat. "Ridiculous story, am I right?"

&emsp;I slowly reached out to it, but she moved back hastily.

&emsp;"Don't touch it!"

&emsp;"Why are you so scared of it then?"

&emsp;She didn't answer. I sighed, anxious.

&emsp;"It's real, isn't it?"

&emsp;"That... That's not..."

&emsp;The beast. The lake. The death. I tensed.

&emsp;"No, no, no, this doesn't make sense. You... you can't be it."

&emsp;"Can't be what?" Her breath was heavy.

&emsp;"... The one we've been..."

&emsp;"It was just a deer, I promise!"

<br><br><br><br>

&emsp;As dark as an absent sun, as sharp as a micrometer blade, I felt my heart sink with my breath. I began to laugh, almost out of insanity. I quieted.

&emsp;"You killed him."

&emsp;"That's not... that's..."

&emsp;"You..."

&emsp;I ran to the house. I didn't say goodbye. I couldn't. I couldn't think. I couldn't as much as sleep.

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">~</span>

&emsp;Friday morning. I woke up in disbelief. I was wrong. Surely. Surely she couldn't be _it_. As soon as I finished eating, I turned on the radio, desperate for something to go off of. I turned to the news as fast as possible. I listened closely.

&emsp;"... word that the police have a warrant out for the arrest of a fisherman named Nickolas McKay, who is suspected to be the feared Lakefront killer."

&emsp;I kept listening.

&emsp;"The police say they have a substantial amount of evidence linking him to the case. However, locals have reported him missing in the past few days. Some say he had been acting strange in the days leading up to his disappearance."

&emsp;A local fisherman began to speak.

&emsp;"Yeah, I've known that man for a long time. Let me tell you, I couldn't get a word of his ramblings. All I made out was somethin bout a box, or a beast or somethin."

&emsp;"Oh, no."

&emsp;My mother walked past.

&emsp;"Is everything alright?"

&emsp;"No."

&emsp;"Is this about last night?"

&emsp;"Yes."

&emsp;"Do you wanna talk about it?"

&emsp;"No."

&emsp;She sighed.

&emsp;"Well, sooner or later, you should talk to her. Okay?"

&emsp;"... Okay."

&emsp;School came and went. I couldn't get it off my mind. It didn't help that I couldn't find her anywhere that day. I asked Mike and Josh if they knew, but they didn't. That afternoon, I searched around the parking lot and found one of her two friends.

&emsp;"Do you know where Jenna is?"

&emsp;"Do I look like I know?"

&emsp;"Yes."

&emsp;"Ugh, no."

&emsp;"Do you know where she lives?"

&emsp;"Ugh, for the last time, I don't know."

&emsp;"You don't know?"

&emsp;"No. She never tells us anything. Look, literally all I know is she rides her bike south coming and north going. That's it."

&emsp;"Well, thanks, anyway."

&emsp;I ran off to the curb and turned northward. It was the same direction she had gone before. I ran down the sidewalk and stopped at a small opening in the bushes. I carefully went down and began to walk around. I tripped on something. I looked down, and at my foot was a deer leg poking out from a dense bush. It was covered in blood. I backed away and out of the area.

&emsp;I walked down the sidewalk a bit further. I stopped and looked across the road. There was a large opening in the trees, with a red brick building situated far back. I crossed over. There was a big sign overtaken by weeds that read "Mooreville Orphanage." I walked a little bit further and looked around. Just behind it was a row of bikes. In the middle was a bike, which had wrapped around it a little lumpy purse.

&emsp;I walked over to the door. It was old, rotting. I knocked. I received no answer, yet the sound of activity still escaped. I slowly reached over and turned the doorknob, to which it did open. In front of me was a dark stairwell, and two halls to my left and my right. I took the left. Though there were many kids, none seemed to notice or care as I wandered around.

&emsp;In the corner were three boisterous and loud boys. I meant to pay them no mind, but they called out.

&emsp;"Hey, new kid!" One said.

&emsp;"What's your name, kid?"

&emsp;"I'm looking for Jenna."

&emsp;"Well, nice to meet you, looking for Jenna!"

&emsp;"Shut up John."

&emsp;I sighed. "Do you know where she is?"

&emsp;"That loser? Upstairs, to the left. Can't miss it."

&emsp;I hastily moved back to the stairwell and went up. I looked to my left, but there was a long hall with many doors. I made my way down. Every door was closed except for the one at the end. A muffled grumble came from inside. I listened closely, and knocked. Nothing. I pushed open the door slowly, and went inside.

&emsp;"Jenna?"

&emsp;I entered, and before me was a beast. It possessed the feathers of an owl, the wings of an eagle, the legs of a lion and the face of a rabid wolf. I scrambled back, but I pushed the door closed behind me. It made its way closer and closer to me, growling heavily. I reached for the door, but I couldn't open it before the beast was hovering inches above me. I looked away.

&emsp;"Please don't kill me!"

&emsp;I reached out my arm. Every breath I took was slow and heavy.

&emsp;"It's just me! It's okay! It's just me!"

&emsp;I closed my eyes for a moment. When I opened them, there was no longer a beast, but Jenna, in the opposite corner, trembling. I stood up slowly.

&emsp;"I'm sorry."

&emsp;I moved a little closer.

&emsp;"I should've listened."

&emsp;I reached out my hand.

&emsp;"Just please let me help you."

&emsp;She slowly grabbed my hand and pulled herself forward. We hugged for a moment, before standing up again.

&emsp;"Who gave you the box?"

&emsp;"I don't know, some guy by the docks. Rambling about something. I can't remember a whole lot." She was quiet, weary.

&emsp;"Some guy by the docks?"

&emsp;"Said his name was Nate or Nick or something..."

&emsp;"Nickolas McKay?"

&emsp;Her eyes widened.

&emsp;"We gotta find him."

&emsp;"No, no, no, that's not good!"

&emsp;"But we have to destroy the box."

&emsp;"We don't need him!"

&emsp;"Your life is at stake."

&emsp;"Yeah but... How could he help me?"

&emsp;"Have you managed to destroy it on your own yet?"

&emsp;She said nothing.

&emsp;"We go down to the docks. If he's there, we press him. Deal?"

&emsp;She stood for a moment.

&emsp;"Deal. But... What if _it_ awakens?"

&emsp;"... I'll find something. I promise."

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">~</span>

&emsp;We wandered around, looking for anyone. The grass was uncut. The wood rotting. An ever unpleasant smell of dead fish lingered in the air. A few docks jutted out from the land, and a few boats sat parked by them. We stopped at a small shack.

&emsp;"I don't see anyone around," I said.

&emsp;"There's usually someone here..."

&emsp;I opened the door to the shack, and we walked in.

&emsp;"Hello?"

&emsp;"Anybody here?"

&emsp;As quick as lightning, a similarly feathered beast appeared in front of the door. It let out a blaring roar. I shut the door without hesitation.

&emsp;"I think we found Nick..."

&emsp;"This isn't good."

&emsp;"It's alright, we can still find a way to..."

&emsp;"_This_ isn't good!"

&emsp;I turned around. She fell on her knees and began to wheeze and cough violently.

&emsp;"Are you okay?"

&emsp;"You need to leave, now!"

&emsp;I hesitated for a moment, but I opened the door, and ran out. I looked back, expecting the beast to follow me, but it stood perfectly still in front of the shack. In an instant, another beast burst through the door of the shack and onto the first. They began swiping and growling at each other. One scraped its claws against the arm of the other, leaving a mark of red. The other wailed in pain.

&emsp;Out of the corner of my eye, I spotted Jenna's purse. I ran over just as the beasts moved past me. They were still focused on each other. I opened the purse, and pulled out the box. The growling ceased. I turned around and they were both staring silently. One of them began to slowly approach.

&emsp;Without much thought, I ran to the edge of the docks, box in hand, and I threw it as far as I could. I watched as it flew and hit the lake with a crash. Out of nowhere, something brushed against my back, and I was hit with a big splash of water. I looked down, where the beast slowly faded among the depths.

&emsp;My heart was racing. I turned around, and I spotted something in the grass. I ran over, to see it was Jenna. For a moment, she lay motionless. She began to cough lightly. She opened her eyes steadily.

&emsp;"Is it gone?"

&emsp;"Yep."

&emsp;I pulled her upright.

&emsp;"That's good."

<span class="odt2md-center-alignment">~</span>

&emsp;I watched as the paramedics put her on the stretcher and loaded it into the ambulance. The sun was beginning to set. I sat on the grass, alone. A man walked up to me. He wore a simple gray suit and fedora. He held out an ID.

&emsp;"Good afternoon. I'm with the police department, if you don't mind answering a few questions."

&emsp;"Sure."

&emsp;"Right. We received a report of an attack, was that you?"

&emsp;"Yes."

&emsp;"Can you describe the attacker to the best of your ability?"

&emsp;"I don't really know... We were out here... Some sort of creature came out of nowhere, knocked her out and disappeared..."

&emsp;"Do you know what it looked like?"

&emsp;"No, it... it happened so fast..."

&emsp;"Anything else that you could describe about it?"

&emsp;"No, not really."

&emsp;"Well, thank you anyway. If you have any questions, just reach out to us, okay?"

&emsp;He handed out a business card with a name, "N. Wusterly - Detective," and a number.

&emsp;"Sure."
