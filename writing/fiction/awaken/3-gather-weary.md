# Awaken, Part 3: Gather, Weary
_by Elliot Kohlmyer, October 21st, 2026_

&emsp;A little key chain. It fell out of the telescope box as I was closing it up. It had a little solar system design and read, "I've been through galaxies and back!" I kept it in my pocket that day, and I completely forgot about it. I intended to attach it to something, but it slipped my mind as my mother came in. Her hands were full of grocery bags, and on top of those was a very delicately placed pizza box.

&emsp;"I got this to heat up for tonight. I hope you don't mind, I'm a little tired."

&emsp;"It's fine. You need any help?"

&emsp;"Nope! I've got it!"

&emsp;She heaved as she set the bags down for a moment.

&emsp;"Are you _really_ sure?"

&emsp;"Oh, alright. Come help me unload these!"

&emsp;I walked over and grabbed a few items.

&emsp;"So, did you make any friends so far?"

&emsp;"In a sense, I suppose."

&emsp;"That's wonderful! I know it's always been hard to adjust."

&emsp;"I, uh, yeah."

&emsp;"You know, you should invite some people over, that always helped me settle in, growing up."

&emsp;"You moved?"

&emsp;"A few times."

&emsp;"Wow, I didn't know that..."

&emsp;"Well now you do!"

&emsp;The next day was more or less the same. Classes were monotonous. Mike and Josh were in my presence whenever I wasn't busy. Even so, I was getting used to it. I looked over briefly at Jenna, in the distance, chatting with her two friends. I tried to distract myself.

&emsp;"So, any news on the killer?" Mike's face lit up like a white neon sign. Well, only for a moment, before settling back to something normal.

&emsp;"Unfortunately, nothing new. But I'm glad to see you're taking an interest!"

&emsp;"Mhmm."

&emsp;"You know what, I actually had a great idea," Josh said.

&emsp;"It's true. He's got some stellar ideas."

&emsp;"I was thinking, we should hold a party! Cause you're the new kid!"

&emsp;"Do they normally do that?"

&emsp;"No, not really. Actually, I just had a greater idea! We should hang out at your house!"

&emsp;"No, I don't think we should."

&emsp;"Oh, come on, it'll be fun!" Mike said. "We could eat food, and do cool stuff, and, you know, I had some better ideas, they just escaped me."

&emsp;"As wonderful as that sounds, I would like to turn down your offer."

&emsp;"But you didn't say you couldn't!"

&emsp;"But I did say we shouldn't!"

&emsp;I looked over to Josh. He was holding his eyelids as wide open as they could go. I assume the intent was to mimic the appearance of a small puppy, but it instead possessed the qualities of a crazed lunatic. Even so, of acceptance or endless pestering, I ultimately chose the former.

&emsp;"Fine, you can come. But only on one condition."

&emsp;"State your terms."

&emsp;I glanced over at Jenna.

&emsp;"You must be content with the additional presence of a... new acquaintance of mine."

&emsp;Mike looked over at Josh. He shrugged. He looked back.

&emsp;"I don't see why not."

&emsp;He pulled a pen and notepad from his pocket.

&emsp;"Alright, now just write down the address and time."

&emsp;He handed me the pen and notepad, and I wrote as much as I could bother to.

&emsp;"Tomorrow night, 6:00 PM sharp. You make it or you break it."

&emsp;"Deal. Oh and sign at the bottom."

&emsp;"I don't know if that's absolutely..."

&emsp;"Sign it."

&emsp;Reluctantly, I signed it and handed the notepad back. He held out his hand and I shook it.

&emsp;Later that day, I saw Jenna behind the school. She was standing alone. I waved quietly from a distance. She waved back. I walked over.

&emsp;"Hi," I said.

&emsp;"Hey."

&emsp;"So uh, are you waiting on someone?"

&emsp;"Oh no, I take my bike here."

&emsp;"Oh okay. Do you wanna hang out at my house?"

&emsp;"Today?"

&emsp;"No, tomorrow night. About dinner."

&emsp;"Sure. Uh, I gotta go."

&emsp;"Really? Okay then, bye."

&emsp;"Bye."

&emsp;She walked over to her bike and took it to the road. I realized I had just invited her over. I had just invited her to hang out with Mike and Josh. No, no, no, I didn't need to worry. I didn't need to care. There wasn't much in my control. I watched as she rode off. A thought entered my mind. A simple question: Why did she ride a bike to school? At worst, why not the bus?

&emsp;I walked over to the edge of the road, and stood. She was still visible, a fair ways in the distance, but visible. She rolled along the tarmac for a bit, but then she stopped. She stopped at the side, and got off very slowly. I wasn't so sure, but she seemed to be approaching something, cautious and careful.

&emsp;I looked to my left, and I saw my mother arrive in the little car. She stopped where I stood.

&emsp;"I see you're ready now? Hop in!"

&emsp;I got in the passenger side, and we headed off.

&emsp;"Anything new?"

&emsp;"Not really. Might have some people over tomorrow."

&emsp;"Oh, that's wonderful dearie! Glad to see you're settling in so fast."

&emsp;"Sure."

&emsp;The radio was playing quiet, but not unnoticed. It was some police statement thing. I didn't bat an eye at first, but the silence of the road compelled me to listen.

&emsp;"... to the conclusion that they are indeed the same. There are simply too many coincident details. Although we've been investigating more, not much else has turned up. Nevertheless we send our condolences to the family of the fisherman, and we are looking to coordinate with farmers over the sightings. We must now ask of you this: If you have any information that can aid in our efforts, please cont..."

&emsp;She reached over and changed the channel to something more musical.

&emsp;"I was fine with the other one."

&emsp;"Oh, sorry, I don't really find that stuff very fun. Not worth the scare anyways."

&emsp;I nodded and rested my head. I looked to my right. We briefly passed an off-road opening, where a bike stood propped up. I looked around a bit, but I couldn't tell if anyone was there.

&emsp;"Did you see something?"

&emsp;"No, thought I did."

&emsp;Thursday night, 6:00 PM. I sat on the couch, reading. My mother was in the kitchen, making dinner. I heard a knock on the door. I forgot about that. I got up and opened it. Mike and Josh were standing there, expressionless. I checked a nearby clock.

&emsp;"How did..."

&emsp;"We got here five minutes early, and we sat outside until the right time."

&emsp;"I'm not sure what we'd have done if we were late."

&emsp;"Well, come in, I suppose."

&emsp;"So, what you got planned, big guy?" Mike asked as they walked in.

&emsp;"Nothing."

&emsp;"Nothing?"

&emsp;"Yep. You can do whatever."

&emsp;"You kids should play a board game," my mother called out from the kitchen. "They're under the coffee table!"

&emsp;"Oh, yeah, that sounds great! Josh? Tom?"

&emsp;"Yeah!"

&emsp;"Eh."

&emsp;"Well it's still two out of three."

&emsp;There was a light knock on the door.

&emsp;"I suppose that is your unnamed acquaintance, no?"

&emsp;"Probably. Although I didn't think she'd actually come..."

&emsp;"She?"

&emsp;I walked over to the door and opened it. Jenna was standing there patiently, reserved.

&emsp;"Hi."

&emsp;"Hey. Come on in."

&emsp;She walked in slowly. Seemingly almost surprised by our abode, at first. She looked over to Mike and Josh, and a faint sly smile briefly formed.

&emsp;"Sup, losers."

&emsp;She looked back to me.

&emsp;"Can I use your bathroom?"

&emsp;"Sure, it's right over there."

&emsp;I turned back to Mike and Josh, who were greatly dismayed.

&emsp;"Your unnamed acquaintance was Jenna?"

&emsp;"_Is_. Look, I told you she isn't as scary as she seems, you'll be fine. Trust me."

&emsp;"Alright, but I'm not counting on it. Something bad's gonna happen tonight."

&emsp;"Something bad indeed."

&emsp;I rolled my eyes. Jenna walked in, with a bit of a grin.

&emsp;"So, you guys got something planned?"

&emsp;"Board game," Mike said stiffly.

&emsp;"Yes," Josh said.

&emsp;"You should ignore them."

&emsp;She nodded apathetically. I walked over to the couch and looked under the coffee table. There were a few games and a deck of cards on top. I took out the cards.

&emsp;"Crazy eights?"

&emsp;"Sick."

&emsp;"Sure."

&emsp;"I don't see why not."

&emsp;I began to deal. Mike and Josh looked tense, but not so much as a moment ago. Jenna was locked on to the cards. Every one I dealt, her eyes followed. She was holding and examining her hand, while Mike and Josh waited until I finished. When all was said and done, I drew the first card. Eight of hearts. I drew again. Four of spades.

&emsp;"Clockwise. That's you, Jenna."

&emsp;She placed a seven of spades.

&emsp;"Is this house rules, by chance?"

&emsp;"Uh, I don't know. Any ideas?"

&emsp;"Ooh, I got one," Josh said. "If you draw a king of hearts, you have to draw eight hundred cards!"

&emsp;"How would that even work?" Mike asked.

&emsp;"You know what, never mind. House rules suck."

&emsp;Jenna let out a small chuckle, and quickly recomposed. My mother walked by with a Polaroid, and took a shot. She shook it, and handed it to me.

&emsp;"Here you go, a memory!"

&emsp;I looked at it for a moment, and set it on the table.

&emsp;At first, the game was slow. Yet, with every card, it intensified. Everyone locked in, hoping for the best or planning with the worst. Mike and Josh became more and more lively over time. Jenna didn't flinch. It seemed she was focused airtight. Every one had a chance of winning at some point, but by the end it felt cyclic and repetitive.

&emsp;"Dinner's ready," my mother called from the kitchen, "I made spaghetti!"

&emsp;"Guys wanna keep playing or..."

&emsp;"I'm quite hungry," Mike said.

&emsp;"Same here," Josh said.

&emsp;"I'm enjoying this," Jenna said. They stared at her intently. "Or we could eat. I'm down for that."

&emsp;We got up and went to the table. We took our servings. Mike and Josh were quick and imprecise. Jenna selected hers slowly and carefully. She put the pasta and the meat sauce in separate places rather than together. She stared at the food with wide eyes, and delicately tasted a bit of meat. Her eyes lit up.

&emsp;"Have you had spaghetti before?"

&emsp;"Yeah, of course. Who hasn't?"

&emsp;"You usually eat them together, by the way."

&emsp;"Oh..." She blushed lightly.
