# Awaken, Part 1: New Kid
_by Elliot Kohlmyer, August 16th, 2026_

&emsp;I didn't expect much from Mooreville. Like every new town, it was old and disconnected from the world around it. Let me put it this way: It was the kind of place elderly people enjoyed dying in. On our way, we saw city skylines erode into historical districts, then high class neighborhoods, then run down backstreets. Before long, endless forest surrounded us. It lasted for ages until eventually, sparse houses and businesses started to appear. Even so, there wasn't much of a semblance of life. Whatever importance this place might've had, I could not see it.

&emsp;The house wasn't much better. But at least it was clean. Old and rickety as it was, there wasn't anything to dust or wipe off, or cause eternal wheezing. For once, it seemed like the previous owners had treated it with care. When I came in, I sat down and stared at the wall. There was a clock. It was afternoon. It was not gloomy outside. My heart jumped when I saw a deer run by the window. It was fast, but it looked rather directionless. At least, that's what little I saw of it, before it disappeared into the wood and bramble.

&emsp;My mother came in shortly after, holding up a box and her Polaroid camera. "Smile," she said enthusiastically. When the picture came out, she handed it to me. I took a look at the rather candid picture of me sitting in a chair. I never learned why, but my mother liked collecting old technology. She always kept a box of old cameras, tape recorders and other things.

&emsp;"About ready to settle in?" She asked as she set down the box.

&emsp;"Ready as I'll ever be," I said with as much gusto as my tired mind could carry.

&emsp;"I know you probably hate moving again, but I really think you'll like it here!"

&emsp;"You said that the last four times."

&emsp;"And this time, I know it's true!"

&emsp;"I'd like to think so."

&emsp;"... Are you alright?"

&emsp;"Well, honestly, no. I just... ugh."

&emsp;"How about you go outside, walk around, get some exercise? We've been on the road for quite awhile."

&emsp;I looked out the window for a moment and saw the warm glow of the sun casting down upon the yard. I didn't have any friends that I could hang out with. I couldn't afford to be much more than a deadbeat, that day. As little as I cared for it, the porch seemed like the best place to go.

&emsp;"I suppose I can do that." I said.

&emsp;My mother carried her box into the little kitchenette around the corner as she spoke.

&emsp;"School starts tomorrow, that ought to keep you busy. Make some friends, make some memories!"

&emsp;"Yeah, I know," I said with a sigh.

&emsp;As I stepped outside, I felt the warm sun battling the cool September breeze. I sat down in a rocking chair, and laid back. The outside of the house was a lot less maintained than the inside, but it wasn't bad. The yard was a bit overgrown, but nothing unreasonable. It slowly started to feel a bit more comfortable.

&emsp;I almost dozed off when I heard some rustling in a nearby bush. Faster than my mind could process, another deer, this time a buck, ran out and off into the woods. I heard movement in the bush again, but when I looked over, nothing more was there. I slowly got up and went over to look closer, but again, nothing. I dug around with my hands, pushing away the leaves and branches. I saw what looked like a pair of yellow eyes beaming through layers of brush, and I fell back.

&emsp;I stood up again and blinked a few times. I was facing just to the side of the house, and there I saw an opening. It seemed that a path led to... somewhere. At first, I couldn't tell what it was. However, the path was not long, and at the end there appeared to be water. I walked along for a few minutes until I came out on the other side, in a little clearing. There it was revealed, the lake.

&emsp;It was small, though it joined with a bigger body of water to the left. Directly opposite to where I stood were little houses which occasionally poked out from the tall trees that lined the edges. To my right, there was a distant dock with boats and what seemed to be a fishing shack. I didn't know exactly why my mother said I would like it here, but I thought maybe this was why. I'll admit that the lake was a nice view. Until now, I had overlooked this place.

&emsp;I woke up early the next morning. Changed my clothes, ate some toast, left the house without really thinking much. We passed through the town on the way to school. It was old, beat up, but I felt like maybe that was a good thing. After moving so much, maybe it wasn't a bad idea to settle somewhere so still and quiet. Everything was where it always was: A local grocery store, a baseball field, a rundown orphanage. Though I tried not to bat an eye, I began to wonder about their stories.

&emsp;A few minutes in, we reached the school. "Good luck, dearie," my mother said as she hugged me. "Remember, make some friends and make some memories!" I stepped out of the car. "I will." I felt obligated to say that I would, but I honestly had no plan to. As the car rolled down the road, I turned to face the school building. It was your average red brick building with a parking lot in front and an American flag stuck on top. It was nothing unfamiliar.

&emsp;Kids filled the lot in groups, and a few of them caught my eye. The jocks were huddled together for some reason, and I noticed three girls chatting unabashedly. Nevertheless, most of my focus was spent on the door ahead of me. I wasn't expecting much as I entered, when a kid started walking beside me. I thought maybe he had a similar route. I stopped. He stopped. I glanced at him briefly, and he appeared to be nervously stationary. I started pacing. He started pacing. I looked at him again, and he held out his hand. I hesitantly shook it.

&emsp;"Mike, how about you?" He asked.

&emsp;"Thomas."

&emsp;"That's a cool name. Are you new here?"

&emsp;"Uh, yeah."

&emsp;"Do you know about the Lakefront Killer?"

&emsp;"What?"

&emsp;"Nothing."

&emsp;"Well this was a productive conversation," I said as I stopped at the classroom door, "but I believe I have class soon."

&emsp;"The bell hasn't even _prepared_ to ring."

&emsp;"I like to be timely."

&emsp;"Fair enough. I'll see you on the other side," he said, walking backwards. Shortly thereafter, he tripped over someone's shoe.

&emsp;I was hoping the classes would feed my mind, but I was wrong. I wish I had recorded Mr Berry's voice, it could've helped greatly with my insomnia. Either that, or a copy of his lectures would've sufficed. The class order wasn't great either. For the morning, there was math, physics \(probably), and what Mr Berry touted as his greatest work, "Latin in Practice." He spent most of that class dissecting the minutia of the school's motto.

&emsp;I was a zombie by the end, but I was out. Now, it was just me, the lunchroom, and an unbroken hallway in between \(Well, there were other students there, but try to keep the vision alive.) As I walked, I could've talked to people, I could've met new faces, but I chose not to. I liked that choice, but I felt a weight to not let my mother down. Either way it didn't matter. As I sat down with my tray of food, Mike immediately spotted me and sat to my left. Another kid sat to my right, and he appeared to be giving hand signals to Mike.

&emsp;"So, Timmy," Mike said.

&emsp;"Thomas."

&emsp;"Right. Do you know about the Lakefront Killer?"

&emsp;"Uh, no."

&emsp;"Do you want to hear about it?" His face lit up.

&emsp;"Sure..." You ought to know I hesitated greatly when I said this. He set his hands on the table and began to speak in a theatrically menacing voice.

&emsp;"It all began one week ago, when wild deer started showing up dead on local farms!"

&emsp;I lifted my brow. He slowly nodded.

&emsp;"They thought it was just a coyote, until one day, everything changed. Old Man Marley went missing by the docks just two days ago. When they found his body..."

&emsp;"This is the crazy part," the other kid said.

&emsp;"... It was slashed and bruised and beaten, unlike ever before!"

&emsp;"Wait, wait, wait... The docks by the lake?"

&emsp;"Good, good, your getting it!"

&emsp;My heart was beating faster than normal. Not incredibly so, but faster.

&emsp;"The doctor couldn't tell what had hit him."

&emsp;"I don't think they would've done the autopsy yet," the other kid chimed in.

&emsp;"Oh, right. The police couldn't tell what had hit him. Some even say, the killer might still be out there, waiting for its next victim..."

&emsp;"Which is extremely likely, considering that it happened two days ago."

&emsp;"Okay, I guess I'll just lay low then?" Between confusion and fear, I wasn't really sure what to say.

&emsp;"Yeah that sounds like a good idea. Oh, by the way, this is Josh."

&emsp;"Sup," he said.

&emsp;"Hey."

&emsp;Three girls passed by at that moment. They were all chuckling heartily, and yet, soullessly.

&emsp;"What a story, Mike," one of them said with a slight malice. The other two were chewing gum. I suppose that's why they didn't say anything. Mike and Josh were visibly shaking, as if they had just seen the Lakefront Killer. However, as the girls moved on, they relaxed.

&emsp;"What... What just happened?" I asked.

&emsp;"Oh, it's only J. E. N. N. A."

&emsp;"Jenna?"

&emsp;"Sssssh!" Josh stared at me intently with his finger held to his lips. "You don't wanna mess with her!"

&emsp;"She's a bad banana!"

&emsp;"Bad news bears, man!"

&emsp;"She just seems like a normal bully to me."

&emsp;"Dude. The jocks are normal bullies. Jenna will rip open your soul and suck the life clean out of it."

&emsp;"I can attest," Josh said. "There was this kid, Elias, who was completely and utterly lifeless after interacting with her _just once_!"

&emsp;"Dude, I think he was lifeless before that."

&emsp;"Well at any rate, you two should stop worrying about her. Seriously. I've seen way worse."

&emsp;"You're really not getting this," Mike sighed. I was about to respond, when I locked eyes with Jenna. In an instant, her piercing gaze almost burned my retinas. Her ice cold, sharp blue eyes stabbed mine a thousand times over. For a brief moment, I didn't move. I couldn't.

&emsp;Before I realized, she was walking towards me, and I saw the world again. I was sitting in the lunchroom. I was sitting next to... _not_ Mike and Josh, who had disappeared. Curiously, Jenna was not with the other two girls any more. She pulled out a seat opposite of me and sat back. She had long straight blonde hair with bangs and a pink headband. She had a little blue purse hung around her shoulder. It had a weird shape, almost as if she had stuffed a bunch of rocks in it. I wasn't given the opportunity to inquire.

&emsp;"Clearly, flirting isn't your thing," she said with a smirk.

&emsp;"Why are you here?"

&emsp;"Same reason as you, newbie."

&emsp;"Really."

&emsp;"Shame you got a seat at the loser table... Looks fitting though!"

&emsp;"Seriously, why are you here? What interest would _you_ have in me?"

&emsp;"Well clearly, you like me. Must be sad, huh? The queen and the peasant."

&emsp;"I hate you." A wild fire began to fester in me.

&emsp;"Whatever you say, kid."

&emsp;"Seriously, I'm not in love with in you or anything like that."

&emsp;"Yeah, we all like to pretend." Every sentence she said was another mile I wanted to run from my seat.

&emsp;"I'm not... For goodness sakes, if I wasn't _blind_, apparently, I would think your parents didn't care about raising you!"

&emsp;Her face went red, and I quieted. She slammed her fist down and stared at me intently. "Don't ever..." She stopped, breathing heavy. She sat back, closed her eyes and recomposed. Her smug look was now worn and apathetic.

&emsp;"You should tryout for Rugby, you'd _really_ enjoy it."

&emsp;"No, I would not." When I looked up, she was gone. She was back with the other two girls, smiling and laughing. I sighed heavily.

&emsp;"Well guys, I concede. Guys?"

&emsp;"Is she gone yet?" Mike whispered from under the table.

&emsp;"Yes. She is gone."

&emsp;"Okay," he said before bumping his head. "Ouch."

&emsp;"Gesundheit," Josh said.

&emsp;The afternoon wasn't much better than the morning. It was mostly the same monotonous voices, and material I could've figured out on my own. Yet, by the end, the first day of school had made a bigger impression on me than I was anticipating. As I recollected, I expected to see my mother pull up in the little car we shared.

&emsp;When I turned the corner, I instead saw something I wished I didn't have to see for the rest of the year. There, behind the school, stood Jenna. This time, she was alone. She was looking around, nervously. Somehow she didn't spot me in the distance. She took a breath. Out of her purse, she pulled a small ornate wooden box. A few seconds later, she threw it to the ground, with the force of a baseball pitch. As it hit the ground, however, it stayed intact. She growled and stomped her foot.

&emsp;At this, I laughed. I laughed loud enough that she noticed me, and I immediately stiffened. Her face went red like a rose, and I expected a scolding. She instead gathered the box and walked as fast as she could in the other direction.
